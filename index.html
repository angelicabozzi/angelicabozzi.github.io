Angelica Bozzi

Welcome! I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Imperial College London, supervised by Sam Asher and Simon Quinn. I study how institutions and technology shape opportunity and access to labor markets, with a focus on development and the economic effects of AI. My work combines field experiments, administrative data, and machine learning. I was a PhD Fellow at Harvard University, hosted by Eliana La Ferrara. I hold a bachelor’s degree from Bocconi University and a master’s degree from Bocconi University and the London School of Economics.

Agricultural Productivity Growth and Women’s Work: Long-Run Evidence from India

With Sam Asher (Imperial College London) and Rebecca Cai (MIT)

Under review

Abstract

This paper asks whether rising rural incomes are driving some of India’s falling female labor force participation. Identification comes from India’s vast network of gravity-driven irrigation canals, which generate exogenous variation in agricultural productivity by distributing water only to nearby lower-elevation villages. We find that canal treatment reduces the employment share in agriculture for both men and women, but labor reallocation differs by gender: men primarily shift toward nonfarm employment while women primarily shift toward unpaid household production. These results show that economic growth may reinforce gender specialization when suitable nonfarm opportunities for women remain limited.
